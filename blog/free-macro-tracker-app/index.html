How to choose a free macro tracker app

Almost every macro tracker is free to download. Fewer stay useful once you start logging, because the parts you use every day, scanning a barcode and setting a protein target in grams, are often the parts behind the subscription. Here’s what to check before you put weeks of logging into one.

Davin Kaster · Published November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

This is Plated’s blog, so we aren’t neutral. Every fact about another app below was read on that app’s US App Store listing or its own website in November 2026, and each one links its source. Apps change their free versions, so check before you commit.

1. Is the barcode scanner free?

Scanning is how most people log packaged food: point the camera at the barcode and the food is in your log, serving size and all. Typing every protein bar and yogurt by hand gets old by the second week, and that’s usually when people stop tracking.

It’s also the feature apps most often move behind a paywall. MyFitnessPal moved barcode scanning to Premium in October 2022 (MyFitnessPal’s announcement), and its App Store listing still lists it as a Premium feature. Lose It! says it is moving its scanner to Premium too, with some free members keeping it during the switch.

Before you sign up, open the app’s App Store listing and look for “barcode” in the list of paid features.

2. Can you set macro goals in grams?

If you lift, your protein target is probably a number of grams: 160 g, 180 g, a gram per pound of goal weight. Some free versions only let you set macros as percentages of your calories, so 30% protein. That sounds the same until your calories change: drop 300 calories for a cut and a percentage target quietly takes protein down with it.

Grams keep the number you chose. On MyFitnessPal, “customized macro, calorie & nutrition goals” are listed as Premium, and the free version sets macros by percentage. Lose It! lists macro and protein targets as Premium.

3. Is there a free version, or just a free trial?

“Free” on the App Store can mean a free download with a trial. MacroFactor, for example, says on its listing that it “has a free trial, but does not offer a free subscription tier.” That’s a fair model if you’re ready to pay, but it isn’t a free macro tracker.

Look for the words “free version” or a free plan on the app’s own pricing page, not just a “Get” button.

4. How fast is it to log a normal day?

The best tracker is the one you still open in a month. Count the taps for the things you do every day: eating the same breakfast again, logging a recipe you cook every week, adding a food the database doesn’t have. Saved meals, your own recipes and copying a previous day save the most time.

Try a full day of real meals during the first week. If logging takes longer than eating, it won’t last.

5. What happens to your training?

If you lift or run, a macro app only tells half the story. Plenty of people end up with one app for food, another for sets and reps, and a third for runs, and the three never talk to each other. That’s three logins, often three subscriptions, and no single place that shows your eating next to your training.

If that’s you, check whether the macro app logs workouts too, and whether that part is free.

6. Can you take your data with you?

Months of logs are worth keeping. Check that you can export your food log, as a CSV file for example, without paying, so switching apps later doesn’t mean starting from zero.

Common questions

Is there a completely free macro tracker?

Several macro trackers have a lasting free version; what differs is which features it includes. Check the barcode scanner and gram macro goals first, since those are the ones most often paid. Plated’s free version includes both.

Should I set macros in grams or percentages?

Grams, if you have a protein target. A percentage moves with your calories, so cutting calories lowers your protein too. A gram goal stays where you set it.

Can I track macros and workouts in the same app?

Yes. Plated logs food and macros, lifting sets with PRs, and GPS runs in one iPhone app, and all three are in the free version.
